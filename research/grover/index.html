GROVER.

Terrain-adaptive grouser wheel

A wheel that changes its tread height while driving. My longest-running research project, GROVER studies how actively changing wheel geometry can improve mobility across different planetary-analog terrains.

Can adjustable grousers reduce slip as terrain changes?

Planetary rovers encounter loose soil, rocks, and firm ground on the same traverse. A fixed wheel must compromise between these surfaces. Taller grousers can improve traction in loose material, but excessive height can increase sinkage and resistance.

GROVER separates propulsion from grouser adjustment, then uses measured slip to search for a better tread height while driving. The experiments ask both whether adjustment helps and when it stops helping.

My work: mechanism invention, mechanical design and structural analysis, fabrication, the instrumented gantry, closed-loop control, and experimental evaluation. Principal investigator: Prof. Yashwanth Nakka.

Experimental results

Reported in the revised author manuscript.

Grouser-height trade-offs

Tests covered vinyl, coarse rock, pea gravel, and loose and dense sand. Excessively tall grousers caused immobilization in sand. More extension was not always better.

Height adjustment during traversal

An instructed change across a terrain boundary reduced slip by 44.0 ± 4.7% from the transient peak across 20 trials.

Slip-feedback adaptation

On four previously uncharacterized terrains, settled slip was 25.4% to 37.2% below the pre-settled mean. The routine finds an improved local configuration, not a guaranteed global optimum.

Control and measured response

These are single-wheel laboratory and field experiments, not space qualification. Continuous rover integration would also require a rotary electrical interface to avoid accumulating servo-cable twist.
