Hardware development

During my research stay, I designed and built docking hardware, combining electromagnet sizing, mechanical CAD, PCB design, and bench demonstrations.

The interface combines magnetic capture, a rotating spline, and mating power and communication boards. The PCB was fabricated after my stay, and bring-up is complete. It is ready for integration by the SIEL researchers. This is a developing prototype, not a finished docking platform.

Hardware testsElectromagnet CAD

Research question

How can two free-floating systems capture, align, and maintain a mechanical and electrical connection?

Docking hardware was built and bench-tested. PCB fabrication and bring-up are complete; integration into the docking system is the next stage of the researchers' work.

Technical notesModel assumptions and specifications

The MATLAB study uses AWG 22 wire, a 3.3 V coil supply, and an effective relative permeability of 100 for the saved design-review example. Its effective magnetic moment is 36.96 A·m². Magnetization is capped using a 1.5 T saturation assumption.

This is a far-field, coaxial dipole estimate, not finite-element analysis. Misalignment, near-contact geometry, actual steel properties, and temperature require hardware validation. The coil supply is separate from the PCB's 12 V / 2 A interface target.

Rotate, align, retain

The interface is designed for a servo to turn the spline and accommodate angular mismatch. Ball plungers retain the coupled halves. The revised groove provides a ratcheting release path, allowing decoupling by rotation instead of continuous magnetic force.

Technical notesRouting checks and archived design files

The displayed board revisions pass KiCad's supplied geometric rules with zero violations and zero unconnected items. The four pogo distribution rings are explicit, net-assigned copper arcs. The 3D views include copper, pads, solder mask, and available component models; the XT30 and TVS footprints are present even though their physical models are unavailable.

A preliminary external-trace sizing screen gives approximately 0.39 mm for 2 A, 2 oz copper, and an assumed 10°C rise, using the formula in TI's PCB layout guidance. This calculation supported the routing work; it is not a measured thermal result or vacuum qualification.

The original 13-component power schematic and the displayed annular-board revisions have matching connectivity among all 33 schematic pins. The schematic specifies TPS259620DDAR; some board values remain generic, and CAN and contact footprints are documented in the layouts. These files document the design work available here, not the final assembly record.

Archived schematic detail. The earlier files shown here connect D2 pin 1 (cathode) to GND and pin 2 (anode) to 12 V, opposite the positive-rail TVS orientation indicated by the Nexperia PTVS18VZ1USK datasheet. Anyone reusing these archived files should check this against the final circuit documentation. This source-file observation does not establish the configuration of the fabricated hardware.
