Research Question

Which parallel leg architectures can support Lunar Leaper, an arachnid-inspired quadruped intended to explore lunar lava tubes by 2030?

Lunar Leaper is a 12U CubeSat-class quadruped concept for lunar exploration. At ETH Zürich, I led the leg-mechanism design, built six full-scale prototypes, and integrated the final system with PMSM actuators.

Mission and design goals

Lunar Leaper is an arachnid-inspired quadruped intended to explore lunar lava tubes by 2030. My task was to explore parallel leg architectures and develop a 3-DOF mechanism within the robot’s packaging, torque, and range-of-motion constraints.

01

Protected, stationary actuators. Keep motors inside the body to limit lunar dust exposure and avoid moving thermal straps and wiring. Account for thermal cycling and launch constraints.

02

Dust-tolerant joints. Evaluate flexures that provide motion without sliding or rolling interfaces requiring joint seals. Compare them with linkage and differential alternatives.

03

Torque and range of motion. Use Simscape Multibody analysis to size the drives and evaluate transmission architectures with heim-joint yaw, hip-pitch joints, and a steel-shaft knee drive.

04

Prototype and integrate. Iterate through six full-scale leg prototypes using rapid fabrication and off-the-shelf hardware, then complete the final prototype’s mechanical and electrical integration.

Mentorship

Many concepts were my original work or developed through brainstorming with my mentor, Joseph “Joe” Church. I designed the CAD and built the prototypes, using established mechanism families where appropriate. Joe taught me a great deal about mechanism design and helped evaluate the alternatives.

Knee transmissions

The first ankle prototype uses a Bowden cable. The final bipedal linkage uses a flexible shaft to rotate a lead screw and pitch the knee joint.

First ankle prototype

Bowden-cable actuation

Review concept · slide 24

A body-mounted actuator pulls a Bowden cable to actuate the final linkage. This prototype was used to assess cable routing, friction, and compliance.

Final integrated design

Bipedal linkage + flex-shaft lead screw

Review concept · slide 23

A flexible rotary shaft, like those used in mechanical tachometers and aircraft systems, transmits rotation from the body-mounted motor to a lead screw. The screw drives the linkage that pitches the final knee joint. The bipedal linkage was developed from the earlier ankle architecture.
