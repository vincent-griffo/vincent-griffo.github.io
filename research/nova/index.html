NOVA.

Collaborative rover platform

A reusable rover for testing autonomy algorithms, with a long-term goal of low-cost, open-source hardware that the lab can build in multiples.

I designed the complete V1 platform mechanically, helped with the electrical architecture, and worked on high-level motion controls. I now mentor the next student team, whose V2 chassis is shown in the CAD and URDF below.

Research platform

Mission and design goals

The aim is a plug-and-play ground platform for the Aerospace Robotics Lab: easy to reproduce, modify, and deploy for autonomy experiments, without rebuilding the vehicle for each algorithm.

In development: lower unit cost, repeatable builds, and an open-source release.

Steer and drive independently.

Four swerve modules, an adapted rocker suspension, and a serviceable chassis.

V2 chassis by the next student team: external Jetson and a hot-swappable battery. Both this model and the driving demo show that iteration.

Steering

Planetary yaw stage

The steering transmission uses a planetary stage with the ring gear as its output to orient the wheel module.

Wheel drive

Belt transmission

A separate gearmotor drives the wheel through a belt. Wheel speed and steering angle can be commanded independently.

Suspension

Rocker articulation

The chassis uses an adapted rocker arrangement to let the wheel modules articulate relative to the body.

Author manuscript

A Lunar Terrain Facility for Robust and Resilient Learning-Based Autonomy

The paper documents NOVA, the lab’s terrain and lighting setup, and autonomy experiments across several robots. NOVA’s trajectory tracking is distinct from the learning and fault-estimation results on Leo Rover. The facility is an Earth-based analog, not a reproduction of lunar conditions.

Y. K. Nakka, V. Griffo, S. Kumar, C. DelGandio, T. Dokania, A. Bhardwaj, S. Kelkar, and U. K. Ponugupati.
