Mechanical harmonic synthesizer

A mechanical demonstration of Fourier synthesis: frequency components with adjustable amplitudes are physically summed to reconstruct a waveform.

My work

I handled the ideation, mechanical design, and all of the CAD. I rebuilt the design around off-the-shelf hardware, MDF, and 3D-printed parts to make the instrument more robust.

Mechanical design

Frequency generation, amplitude adjustment, and summation.

Harmonic Analyzer V2
3D assembly
01

Generate different frequencies

Different gear ratios produce different frequencies from a common drive. The gear train supplies the repeating motions used to build the waveform.

02

Set each amplitude

The amplitude of each component sets how much it contributes to the output. Adjusting these contributions changes the resulting waveform.

03

Sum and plot

The mechanism adds the individual motions and transfers their sum to the pen. This is analog waveform reconstruction using a finite set of frequency components.

Fabrication and demonstration

Square-wave reconstruction

In live demonstrations, we combined frequency components at selected amplitudes to approximate a square wave mechanically.

With a finite set of components, the output approximates a square wave rather than reproducing its sharp transitions exactly.

Design and inspiration

I created the full CAD and mechanical redesign, drawing substantial inspiration from the Harmonic Analyzer: Mechanical Lasercut Signal Plotter . The design was rebuilt for a more robust combination of commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) hardware, MDF structure, and 3D-printed components.
